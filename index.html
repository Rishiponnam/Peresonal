Study Strategy

How to Study for SSC CGL — Complete Beginner Guide

A step-by-step strategy from Day 1 to exam day. Understand the exam, build a foundation, practice, and peak at the right time.

Step-by-Step Strategy
1

Understand the Exam Pattern First

Before touching any book, fully understand what you are preparing for. Read the SSC CGL notification. Know the 4 subjects, 25 questions each, 50 marks each, 60 minutes total, −0.5 for every wrong answer. This context helps you make smarter decisions about what to study and what to skip.

2

Take a Baseline Mock on Day 1 (No Preparation)

Sit for one free mock test on Testbook or Adda247 without any preparation. Note your score per subject. This is your starting point. Most beginners score 70–100 out of 200 — that is completely normal. Now you know which subjects need the most work.

3

Month 1 — Build Foundation Only

Start with NCERT Class 6–10 for GA (History, Geography, Polity, Science). For Maths, master only arithmetic — LCM/HCF, Percentage, Profit/Loss, SI/CI, Ratio, Average. For English, learn all 12 tenses and basic grammar rules. For Reasoning, do easy topics: analogy, coding, series. Do NOT attempt advanced topics yet.

4

Rotate Subjects — One Per Time Slot

Never study the same subject for more than 60 minutes in one stretch. Your brain's retention drops sharply after 60 minutes of the same topic. Rotate: Morning → Maths, Evening → English or Reasoning, Night → GA. This spaced approach improves retention by 40%.

5

Maintain 3 Registers — Non-Negotiable

Formula Register: Write every new formula as you learn it. Review weekly. Vocabulary Register: 10 words per day with meaning, synonym, antonym. Mistake Register: Every wrong question from mocks — write the question, your wrong answer, and the correct approach. Re-solve weekly.

6

Start Mock Tests from Week 4 — Not Later

Most beginners delay mocks thinking "I'll start mocks after I finish studying." This is a big mistake. Start mocks from Week 4 even if preparation is incomplete. Mocks reveal exactly what you don't know, what you know but can't solve in time, and how to manage 60 minutes strategically under pressure.

7

Analyze Every Mock — This is the REAL Learning

Spending 3 hours giving a mock but only 10 minutes analyzing is wasted effort. For every mock, spend equal time analyzing: Which questions were wrong? Was it a concept gap or a silly mistake? Which topics need revision this week? This analysis drives real improvement — not the mock itself.

8

Solve Previous Year Questions from Day 50

SSC repeats concepts and sometimes near-identical questions year after year. Solving 2018–2024 PYQs is the most important practice you can do. Focus on Maths PYQs first (patterns are clear), then GA (direct fact questions repeat), then Reasoning. Sources: Testbook free PYQs, Adda247, or Kiran PYQ book.

9

15 Minutes of Current Affairs Every Morning

GA accounts for 50 marks and includes events from the last 6–12 months before the exam. Spend 15 minutes every morning reading current affairs — GKToday daily MCQs or AffairsCloud. In 90 days this gives you 1,350 minutes = 22.5 hours of GA preparation without any special effort.

10

Last 15 Days — Zero New Topics

In the last 15 days before the exam: no new topics, no new books. Only revision of what you have studied. Re-solve your mistake register. Light mocks every 2 days. Sleep 8 hours every night — your brain consolidates memory during deep sleep. Eat well and stay calm. Your preparation is done; trust it.

Do's & Don'ts

✅ Always Do This

✓ Take mocks every week from Week 4 onwards
✓ Analyze every mock thoroughly the next day
✓ Learn 10 vocabulary words every single day
✓ Revise previous week's topics every Sunday
✓ Use LCM method for Time & Work problems
✓ Maintain formula, vocab, and mistake registers
✓ Attempt Reasoning first in the actual exam
✓ Sleep 7–8 hours daily — non-negotiable

❌ Never Do This

✗ Skip mock analysis — it's half the preparation
✗ Neglect English — it's the easiest 50 marks
✗ Mug GA facts without understanding context
✗ Switch books mid-preparation — complete one first
✗ Randomly guess — −0.5 per wrong answer hurts
✗ Attempt Maths before Reasoning in the exam
✗ Start new topics in the last 2 weeks before exam
✗ Sacrifice sleep — memory consolidation needs rest

📌 LCM & HCF — Concepts, Formulas and Shortcuts

▾
📐 Core Formulas
HCF × LCM = Product of the two numbers (a × b)
HCF of fractions = HCF of Numerators ÷ LCM of Denominators
LCM of fractions = LCM of Numerators ÷ HCF of Denominators
⚡ Powerful Shortcuts
→ If HCF of two numbers is H and their ratio is a:b → numbers are aH and bH
→ Smallest number divisible by x, y, z = LCM(x, y, z)
→ Largest number that divides a, b, c leaving same remainder r = HCF(a−r, b−r, c−r)
→ Largest number that divides a, b, c leaving remainders p, q, r = HCF(a−p, b−q, c−r)
📋 Classic Example
Q: HCF of two numbers is 12, LCM is 360. One number is 60. Find the other.
Other number = (HCF × LCM) ÷ First number = (12 × 360) ÷ 60 = 4320 ÷ 60 = 72

📌 All Formulas, Rules and Conversions

▾
📐 Core Formulas
Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100
x% of y = y% of x (very useful shortcut)
% Change = [(New Value − Old Value) ÷ Old Value] × 100
If A is x% more than B → B is less than A by: [x ÷ (100+x)] × 100 %
If A is x% less than B → B is more than A by: [x ÷ (100−x)] × 100 %
Successive % change (x% then y%): Net = x + y + (x×y÷100)
Population after n years = P × (1 ± r/100)ⁿ

Q: Price increased by 20%, then decreased by 10%. What is net % change?
Net = 20 + (−10) + (20 × −10 ÷ 100) = 10 − 2 = +8% net increase

Profit & Loss High Priority
2–3 questions guaranteed in every SSC CGL paper. The dishonest dealer trick and the two-items-same-SP trap appear almost every year.

📌 All Formulas and High-Frequency Shortcuts

▾
📐 Core Formulas
Profit = SP − CP | Loss = CP − SP
Profit% = (Profit ÷ CP) × 100 | Loss% = (Loss ÷ CP) × 100
SP = CP × (100 + Profit%) ÷ 100
SP = CP × (100 − Loss%) ÷ 100
CP = SP × 100 ÷ (100 + Profit%) or CP = SP × 100 ÷ (100 − Loss%)
Discount% = (MP − SP) ÷ MP × 100
SP after two discounts d1 and d2 = MP × (100−d1)/100 × (100−d2)/100

⚡ SSC-Favourite Shortcuts — Appear Every Year!
→

→
Two items sold at same SP, one at x% profit, other at x% loss → ALWAYS a LOSS. Loss% = x² ÷ 100
→ If SP of x items = CP of y items → Profit% = (y−x) ÷ x × 100
→ If article sold at profit of a% instead of loss of b% → difference in SP = (a+b)% of CP
📋 Classic SSC Trap Question

Avoid These

Common Mistakes to Avoid in SSC CGL

These mistakes cost students 10–25 marks each year. Know them, recognize them in yourself, and eliminate them systematically.

📋 Preparation Mistakes
1

Starting with advanced topics before basics are strong

Many students start Geometry and Trigonometry when their Percentage and Profit/Loss are still weak. Arithmetic topics (1–10) carry 15–18 marks in Tier 1. Master them completely before moving to advanced maths.

2

Ignoring English because "I can manage"

English is the easiest 50 marks in SSC CGL. 5–10 dedicated days on grammar rules (tenses, SVA, voice) and vocabulary can add 8–12 marks. Yet most students ignore it until the last week. Don't make this mistake.

3

Not maintaining a Mistake Register

Getting a mock question wrong but not writing it down means you will repeat that exact mistake in the exam. Every wrong answer must go into your Mistake Register with the correct approach written beside it. Re-solve it weekly.

4

Switching books frequently

Students who switch from R.S. Aggarwal to Rakesh Yadav to Kiran mid-preparation retain nothing from any. Pick ONE book per subject and complete it fully — every exercise, every chapter — before even opening another book.

5

Taking mocks without analysis

Taking 30 mocks and scoring the same 110 every time means you are practicing mistakes, not correcting them. After every mock, spend at least as much time analyzing as you spent giving the mock itself. That analysis is the real learning.

6

Delaying mock tests until "preparation is complete"

Preparation is never "complete." Students who delay mocks lose the benefit of time — mocks reveal gaps that only practice under exam conditions exposes. Start mocks from Week 4. Your score will be low. That is expected and useful.

📝 Exam-Day Mistakes
7

Random guessing with no strategy

With −0.5 per wrong answer, guessing randomly is dangerous. Only attempt a question if you can confidently eliminate at least 2 of the 4 options. When all 4 seem equally plausible, skip it entirely and return if time allows.

8

Not practicing under 15-minute sectional timers

With the 2026 sectional timing change, you have exactly 15 minutes per section — the timer locks automatically. If you waste 4 minutes on one hard question, you lose 4 minutes from 25 questions. Practice every subject under a strict 15-minute timer from Day 1. Timing yourself is now the single most important exam skill.

9

Attempting questions in order (Q1, Q2, Q3...)

Never attempt questions in serial order. Scan each section quickly in 90 seconds, mark the easy questions, solve those first, then go back to harder ones. Cherry-pick easy questions in each section to maximize marks in minimum time.

10

Misreading questions due to haste

SSC questions often have wording traps: "how much MORE than", "ratio of B to A (not A to B)", "how many times less." Read every question carefully twice before beginning the calculation. Most wrong answers in mocks come from misreading, not wrong methods.

✅ Golden Rules for Exam Day (2026)

✓ Each section = 15 min fixed. Decide attempt count beforehand.
✓ Never spend more than 35–40 seconds on any question
✓ Scan section in first 30 seconds, pick easiest 20 questions first
✓ If unsure about a question, skip instantly and come back
✓ Read each question TWICE before calculating
✓ If time is left in a section, review marked answers — not move on
✓ Tier 1 is qualifying only — stay cool, just clear the cut-off

❌ Things to Strictly Avoid (2026)

✗ Don't spend 2+ min on any single question — move on instantly
✗ Don't expect to "save" time from GA for Maths — sections are locked
✗ Don't attempt in Q1→Q25 order — cherry-pick easy ones first

📌 Multiply Any 2-Digit Numbers Mentally

📐 Multiply Near 100 (Base Method)
Step 1: Write how far each number is from 100 (deficiency)
Step 2: Answer = (Either number − other's deficiency) | (Product of deficiencies)
Example: 97 × 96
Deficiencies: 97 is 3 below 100. 96 is 4 below 100.
Left part: 97 − 4 = 93. Right part: 3 × 4 = 12. Answer: 9312
📐 Multiply by 11 — Instant Method
For 2-digit number AB: Answer = A | (A+B) | B

📌 Mental Math for Number System Questions

▾
📐 Digit Sum Trick (for divisibility and remainders)
Digit sum of any number = sum of all digits repeatedly until single digit
Ex: 3456 → 3+4+5+6=18 → 1+8=9. Digit sum = 9. So 3456 is divisible by 9.
Remainder when divided by 9 = digit sum remainder. (3457÷9: digit sum=10→1+0=1. Remainder=1)
📐 Units Digit Pattern (Cyclicity)
Powers of 2: units digits cycle as 2,4,8,6 (period 4)
Powers of 3: units digits cycle as 3,9,7,1 (period 4)
Powers of 4: cycle 4,6 (period 2). Powers of 7: cycle 7,9,3,1 (period 4)
Powers of 5: always ends in 5. Powers of 6: always ends in 6.
To find units digit of 7⁴⁵: 45÷4=11 remainder 1. So units digit = 7¹ = 7
